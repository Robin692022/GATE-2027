Strategic Execution Summary

Maintain a sustainable, revision-led preparation rhythm. Ensure short formula summaries are completed immediately after every chapter, with regular PYQ practice and mistake log maintenance.

Phase 1 - Momentum and SOM Completion
25 July - 15 August | Main objective: finish Strength of Materials properly and restart a stable study rhythm.
  • Primary work: continue PW from torsion in circular shafts. Complete torsion, springs, columns/struts, pressure vessels, combined loading, strain energy, and any remaining course modules.
  • After each chapter: make a maximum two-page formula summary; solve chapter-wise PYQs; tag questions as correct but slow, concept error, formula/application error, or careless.
  • End target: SOM notes complete, a formula-register section, chapter-wise PYQs attempted, and one mixed SOM subject test.
  • Maintenance: retain Mathematics, Basic Thermodynamics, and Applied Thermodynamics through the weekly rotation in Section 4.
Phase 2 - Core Mechanics
16 August - 30 September | Build the mechanics base without wasting time repeating Engineering Mechanics.
  • Engineering Mechanics first (about 1 week, YouTube): finish centroid; finish moment of inertia if pending; complete kinetics of rigid bodies. Then do PYQs and make a one-page formula/concept sheet. Do not restart statics or particle kinetics.
  • Theory of Machines & Vibrations next (PW full course): mechanisms, kinematics, velocity/acceleration, gears, governors, gyroscope, balancing, and vibrations. Solve PYQs after each unit, not at the end.
  • End target: Engineering Mechanics closed, TOM completed once with PYQs, SOM kept alive through maintenance revision.
Phase 3 - Fluids and Manufacturing
1 October - 10 November | Complete two major, high-return areas while earlier subjects remain active.
  • Fluid Mechanics & Machinery (PW): fluid statics; Bernoulli and energy equation; flow measurement; laminar/turbulent flow; pipe flow; boundary layer; turbomachines. Build concept maps for assumptions and relation families.
  • Manufacturing Process 1 & 2 (PW): casting, welding, forming, machining, metrology/production methods as relevant to your batch, powder metallurgy, and non-traditional machining. Build comparison tables, not lengthy prose notes.
Phase 4 - Design, Management, and Compressed Subjects
11 November - 10 December | Finish the remaining high-return subjects; do not turn lower-return subjects into a time sink.
  • Machine Design (PW): shafts, keys, springs, bearings, riveted and welded joints, and the applicable syllabus modules. Pair every related formula set with a short SOM refresh.
  • Industrial Engineering (PW): inventory, forecasting, CPM/PERT, queuing, time study, work sampling, operations research/standard models as covered. Practice standard question patterns repeatedly.
  • Material Science (YouTube, concise): 10-15 hours plus PYQs; aim for recognition, diagrams, and common facts rather than exhaustive theory.
  • General and Verbal Aptitude: regular PYQs/practice, with targeted quant lessons only if scores expose a real weakness.
  • Heat Transfer (selective): conduction, fins, radiation basics, heat exchangers. If time remains, add transient conduction and phase-change basics.
Phase 5 - Consolidation
11 December - 31 December | No planned new major subject. Turn studied once into retrievable under pressure.
  • Perform subject-wise revision using the formula register, short notes, marked PYQs, and mistake register - not full lectures.
  • Run weekly sectional tests and begin one full-length mock per week once a reasonable syllabus base is available.
  • Give special attention to early subjects: SOM, Thermodynamics, Mathematics, and TOM.
Phase 6 - Final Attack
January - examination | No new resources. No routine new lectures. Score improvement now comes from analysis and recall.
  • Take roughly two full-length mocks per week, aiming for 10-12 quality mocks before the examination. Analyze each at least as long as you wrote it.
  • Revise formulas daily; review the mistake register weekly; reattempt marked PYQs and weak-topic sets.
  • In the final days, reduce novelty. Use familiar notes, formula sheets, confidence-building PYQs, sleep, and a stable routine.
